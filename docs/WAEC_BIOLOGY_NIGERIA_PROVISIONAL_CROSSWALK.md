# WAEC WASSCE Biology — provisional Nigeria crosswalk

**Working reference:** [WAEC Biology 2026/2027](https://waecsyllabus.com/biology/)  
**Source status:** Secondary reproduction of WAEC syllabus text; direct primary-source PDF verification remains pending.  
**Candidate scope:** Section A (common) plus Section C (Nigeria, Sierra Leone, The Gambia and Liberia). Do not teach Section B as Nigerian WAEC content; that section is identified as Ghana-only in the working reference.

## Section A — common syllabus

| WAEC topic | Required content to evidence | Existing lesson mapping |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Concept of Living | Classification and kingdoms; levels of organisation; unicellular, colonial, filamentous and multicellular forms; cell structure and function; diffusion, osmosis, active transport; cell nutrition, respiration, excretion, growth, development, movement, reproduction; tissues/support; transport; respiratory/excretory systems; homeostasis; hormonal/nervous coordination; sense organs; reproduction in animals/plants. | `bio-cell`, `bio-classification`, `bio-transport`, `bio-respiration`, `bio-excretion`, `bio-support`, `bio-reproduction`, `bio-growth`, `bio-coordination` |
| 2. Plant and Animal Nutrition | Photosynthesis, mineral requirements, food classes/sources, balanced diet, food tests, digestive enzymes, modes of nutrition, alimentary systems, dental formula and feeding in protozoa/mammals. | `bio-nutrition` |
| 3. Basic Ecological Concepts | Ecosystems and components; ecological factors and their measurement; trophic levels, food webs and energy flow; decomposition; biological associations; adaptation to habitats; pollution; succession; population factors; microorganisms, disease, public health and food preservation. | `bio-ecology-factors`, `bio-interactions`, `bio-habitats`, `bio-biomes`, `bio-populations`, `bio-environment` |
| 4. Conservation of Natural Resources | Conservation of soil, water, wildlife, forests and minerals; practical methods for sustainable use. | `bio-environment`, `bio-soil` |
| 5. Variation in Population | Morphological and physiological variation: size, height, weight, colour, fingerprints, tongue rolling, PTC tasting and ABO blood groups. | `bio-variation` |
| 6. Biology of Heredity (Genetics) | Genetic terminology; transmission/expression of traits; Mendel's experiments/laws; chromosome basis of heredity; probability and hybrid formation; linkage, sex determination and sex-linked traits; applications in agriculture and medicine. | `bio-heredity` |
| 7. Adaptation for Survival and Evolution | Behavioural adaptation in social animals, termites and bees; evidence and theories of evolution. | `bio-adaptation`, `bio-evolution-theory`, `bio-evolution-evidence` |

## Examination structure in the working reference

- Paper 1: 50 multiple-choice questions from Section A, 50 marks, 50 minutes.
- Paper 2: essay/structured questions drawn from Section A and the national section. Nigerian candidates answer Section A and compulsory Section C items.
- Paper 3: practical for school candidates or practical-work test for private candidates. Section A practical questions and the relevant national Section C practical question are required.

The exact paper scheme and mark allocation must be confirmed against the direct WAEC PDF before being hard-coded into an exam timer or marking engine.

## Implementation completed in MeritPrep 2.0

A WAEC Nigeria-specific activity layer has been embedded in relevant Biology lessons:
- cell theory, irritability, taxis and nastic response classification;
- chemosynthetic/carnivorous plants and specialised feeding modes;
- housefly complete metamorphosis, courtship and embryo nourishment;
- trophic energy transfer with a warning that the 10% rule is approximate;
- ecological tolerance range with explicit minimum, optimum and maximum;
- aquatic/terrestrial habitat conditions and adaptations;
- population density from sample count and area;
- microorganisms, food/water hygiene, vectors and public health;
- ABO red-cell compatibility with Rh/crossmatch and paternity limitations;
- structural adaptation, colouration and competition;
- agricultural practices and ecological consequences.
