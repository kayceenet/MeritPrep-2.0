**Status:** Working syllabus map; not an approved or independently verified syllabus transcription.  
**Scope:** Nigeria candidates; common Section A plus Nigeria-specific Section C.

## Examination structure represented in the source copy

- Paper 1: 50 objective questions from the common Section A; 1 hour; 50 marks.
- Paper 2: 2-hour theory paper; 10 compulsory short structured questions from Section A (25 marks), followed by Section B with four questions, two from the common section and two from the candidate's country-specific section; candidates answer any three of the four (75 marks).
- Paper 3: 2-hour practical for school candidates or 1 hour 30 minutes alternative-to-practical for private candidates; three compulsory questions, 50 marks. The source specifies one quantitative-analysis question, one qualitative-analysis question and one question on familiarity with practical activities.

## Common Section A — all candidates

The following fifteen syllabus headings form the common content map:

1. **Introduction to Chemistry**
   - Measurement of mass, length, time, temperature and volume; SI units; significant figures; precision and accuracy; scientific method.
2. **Structure of the Atom**
   - Atomic models; atomic and mass numbers; isotopes; relative atomic and molecular mass; atoms, molecules and ions; electron configurations of the first 30 elements; orbitals and electron-filling principles.
3. **Standard Separation Techniques for Mixtures**
   - Mixture classification; crystallisation, distillation, precipitation, magnetisation, chromatography and sublimation; purity by melting and boiling points.
4. **Periodic Chemistry**
   - Periodic law, classification and trends; third-period gradation; selected metal reactions; halogen trends and displacement; first transition series.
5. **Chemical Bonds**
   - Ionic, covalent, coordinate and metallic bonding; Lewis structures; molecular shapes; intermolecular forces; hydrogen bonding; properties explained by bonding.
6. **Stoichiometry and Chemical Reactions**
   - Symbols, formulae, equations, empirical and molecular formulae; laws of chemical combination; mole, Avogadro constant, mole ratios, solutions, concentration and dilution.
7. **States of Matter**
   - Kinetic theory; changes of state; diffusion and Brownian motion; gas laws; ideal-gas equation; gas preparation and properties; vapour pressure; solid structures and properties.
8. **Energy and Energy Changes**
   - Energy and enthalpy; exothermic and endothermic processes; combustion, dissolution and neutralisation.
9. **Acids, Bases and Salts**
   - Acid/base concepts; properties and strength; electrolytes; pH; salts and hydrolysis; hygroscopic, deliquescent and efflorescent substances; indicators and acid-base titration.
10. **Solubility of Substances**
    - Solubility, saturation, solubility curves, temperature effects, crystallisation/recrystallisation, qualitative-analysis applications and calculations.
11. **Chemical Kinetics and Equilibrium System**
    - Reaction rate; factors and collision theory; activation energy; rate graphs; reversible reactions, dynamic equilibrium and Le Chatelier's principle.
12. **Redox Reactions**
    - Oxidation/reduction, oxidation states, oxidising/reducing agents, balancing redox equations, electrochemical cells, electrode potentials, e.m.f., electrolysis, Faraday's laws and corrosion.
13. **Chemistry of Carbon Compounds**
    - Organic classification, functional groups, nomenclature, purification, petroleum, empirical/molecular formulae, homologous series, isomerism, alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, benzene, alkanols, alkanoic acids and esters.
14. **Chemistry, Industry and the Environment**
    - Local resources and industries, industrial raw materials and siting, industrial effects, air/water/soil pollution, greenhouse effect, ozone depletion, biodegradability and fermentation/biotechnology.
15. **Basic Biochemistry and Synthetic Polymers**
    - Proteins, amino acids, fats/oils, saponification, carbohydrates, reducing-sugar tests, starch, monomers, polymers, polymerisation, thermoplastics and thermosets.

## Nigeria-specific Section C

Section C in the reviewed copy is labelled for candidates in Nigeria, Sierra Leone, Liberia and The Gambia. MeritPrep's Nigeria track should map the following content, then verify the Nigeria-specific examination scope against the current WAEC-issued document.

1. **Non-metals and Their Compounds**
   - Carbon allotropes; coal and destructive distillation; coke, water gas and producer gas; carbon oxides and carbonates; oxygen and oxides; hydrogen; water, hardness and treatment; halogens and chlorine; hydrogen chloride; nitrogen, ammonia, nitric acid and nitrates; sulphur and its compounds; noble gases.
2. **Metals and Their Compounds**
   - Extraction of aluminium, iron and tin; alloys; sodium and its compounds; calcium and its compounds; reactivity of iron and aluminium; copper and its compounds.

## Practical competencies

### General skills and principles
- Measure mass and volume accurately.
- Prepare and dilute standard solutions.
- Filter, recrystallise and determine melting points.
- Measure heats of neutralisation and solution.
- Determine pH by colorimetric methods.
- Determine reaction rates from concentration-time data.
- Investigate simple equilibrium systems.

### Quantitative analysis
- Acid-base titration using appropriate standard solutions and indicators.
- Calculate concentration, molar mass, water of crystallisation, solubility and percentage purity.
- Apply mole ratios and reaction stoichiometry.
- Redox titrations: acidified permanganate with iron(II), acidified permanganate with oxalate, and iodine/iodide with thiosulphate.

### Qualitative analysis
- Cation tests with aqueous sodium hydroxide and aqueous ammonia: NH₄⁺, Ca²⁺, Pb²⁺, Cu²⁺, Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺, Al³⁺ and Zn²⁺.
- Anion tests and confirmatory tests for chloride, sulphite, carbonate, nitrate and sulphate.
- Halogen comparison and displacement reactions.
- Gas tests for hydrogen, ammonia, carbon dioxide, hydrogen chloride and sulphur dioxide.
- Functional-group tests for alkenes, alkanols, alkanoic acids, reducing sugars, starch and proteins.

## Implemented practical learning components (MeritPrep alpha)\n\n- **Solution concentration:** adjustable NaCl mass and final solution volume with molarity output.\n- **Acid-base titration:** HCl–NaOH 1:1 reaction; learner supplies standard acid concentration, mean titre and NaOH aliquot; the app calculates the unknown NaOH concentration and shows the equation. A pure calculation engine has unit tests.\n- **Qualitative cation reference:** selected cations tested with aqueous NaOH or aqueous ammonia, including excess-reagent behaviour and the warmed ammonium test. A pure reference engine has unit tests.\n- **Structured theory practice:** six original tasks with mark totals, answer-writing space, indicative marking points and examiner guidance. Learners can reveal the guide after attempting a response and self-check included points.\n\nThese components are teaching simulations/reference exercises, not a complete WAEC Paper 3 simulator. They do not assess burette reading, endpoint judgement, concordance, safe physical technique or every required qualitative test. The current cation scheme is deliberately limited and must not be used as a universal identification key.\n\n## Content and safety rules

- Mark all newly authored material as original MeritPrep practice content, not official WAEC past questions.
- Keep syllabus source, rights/provenance, author, reviewer, version and approval status with each content object.
- Do not publish draft chemistry explanations, answer keys or practical observations as academically approved.
