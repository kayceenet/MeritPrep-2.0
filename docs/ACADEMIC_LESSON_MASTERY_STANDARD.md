# MeritPrep 2.0 Academic and Instructional Standard
Version 1.0 — Mandatory for all JAMB/WAEC science content

## 1. Mission and definition of success
MeritPrep must teach a learner from zero assumed prior knowledge to independent, examinable mastery of every current JAMB and WAEC syllabus requirement. The target is demonstrable syllabus mastery, not a guarantee of a particular examination score. Content quantity, lesson titles, videos watched or questions attempted do not prove mastery.

A syllabus requirement is complete only when its learning outcomes are taught, appropriately visualised or modelled, practised, assessed and supported by remediation. Practical requirements must include practical competence, not only written descriptions.

## 2. Authority, syllabus audit and traceability
Use current official JAMB and WAEC syllabus documents as the source of truth. Record document title, issuing body, version/session/year, URL or repository source, date checked and any ambiguity. Do not invent requirements or assume a topic is included because it appears in a textbook.

Create a traceability row for EACH syllabus statement/subtopic. Required fields:
- Exact syllabus wording and source/version.
- Subject, topic, subtopic and unique internal lesson ID.
- Prerequisites and vocabulary.
- Measurable learning outcomes.
- Lesson explanation and worked examples.
- Diagram/animation/model reference.
- Interactive simulation/virtual lab/visual activity, or a documented pedagogical reason why none is appropriate.
- Practical skills and safety requirements where relevant.
- JAMB objective coverage and WAEC theory/practical coverage.
- Misconceptions, remediation and retrieval schedule.
- Evidence links, reviewer, status and last review date.

Statuses must distinguish: NOT AUDITED, GAP, IN DEVELOPMENT, IMPLEMENTED, ASSESSED, MASTERY-READY and VERIFIED. Never infer 100% coverage from a count of lessons or questions.

## 3. Zero-to-mastery lesson architecture
Each substantial lesson must be designed as a progression. Adapt the sequence to the concept, but do not omit a stage without documenting why it is not relevant.

### A. Orientation and diagnostic
- Start with a familiar event, real-world problem, demonstration or question.
- State what the learner will be able to explain/do by the end.
- Identify prerequisite knowledge and teach missing foundations.
- Diagnose vocabulary, numeracy, units, algebra, graph reading or earlier science concepts needed.
- Never shame a beginner or assume prior exposure.

### B. Foundational explanation
- Explain new terms in simple, accurate language before using them extensively.
- Build from concrete examples to abstract ideas.
- Explain what the concept means, why it matters, how it works and where it applies.
- Distinguish similar terms and concepts explicitly.
- Introduce formal definitions and accepted scientific terminology.
- Identify prerequisites, exceptions, conditions and boundaries.

### C. Deep scientific treatment
As appropriate to the topic, include:
- Accurate definitions, principles, laws, theories and classifications.
- Mechanisms and causal sequences, not memorised statements alone.
- Relevant structures, components, functions and relationships.
- Equations, derivations, symbols, units, dimensions and sign conventions.
- Assumptions, idealisations, exceptions and limitations.
- Connections to preceding and subsequent syllabus topics.
- Worked examples progressing from simple to multi-step and unfamiliar contexts.
- Common errors, why they occur and how to avoid them.

### D. Visual and multimodal teaching
Choose the most useful representation: labelled static diagram, annotated sequence, animation, 2D/3D model, molecular view, ray diagram, circuit, force diagram, graph, timeline, classification key, specimen view or process flow.
- Labels must be scientifically correct, legible and unambiguous.
- Visuals must teach relationships, structure, scale, sequence or causality.
- Provide pause, replay, stepwise inspection or user-controlled exploration where useful.
- Pair essential visual information with explanatory text; do not rely on colour alone.
- Do not use decorative animation that does not teach.
- Avoid overcrowding; split complex systems into progressive layers.

### E. Worked reasoning and guided practice
- Show each reasoning step and explain why it is taken.
- Explain formula selection, substitution, unit conversion, significant figures and answer interpretation.
- Move from fully worked examples to faded/partially completed examples and then independent tasks.
- Include hints that reveal reasoning progressively, not merely the answer.
- Include conceptual, numerical, diagrammatic, data-based and application questions where relevant.

### F. Inquiry and interaction
Where a simulation is suitable, require the learner to:
1. Predict an outcome and give a reason.
2. Choose or adjust meaningful variables.
3. Run or step through the model.
4. Observe visual and numerical outcomes.
5. Compare prediction with observation.
6. Explain the result using the scientific principle.
7. Explore a changed condition or boundary case.
8. Answer a transfer question in a new context.

A control is meaningful only if its effect is scientifically coherent and explained. Show units and valid ranges. Graphs, animations and numerical outputs must agree.

### G. Retrieval, transfer and examination readiness
- Include short recall checks without notes.
- Revisit prior concepts through spaced retrieval and cumulative quizzes.
- Include questions that require transfer to unfamiliar examples.
- Train examination command words: define, state, list, describe, explain, compare, distinguish, calculate, determine, sketch, draw, label, deduce, infer, discuss and evaluate.
- Provide JAMB-style timed objective practice and WAEC-style structured, essay, calculation, diagram and practical/data tasks as appropriate.
- Explain correct answers, distractors, marking points and common reasons marks are lost.

## 4. Virtual laboratory standard
A virtual lab is a guided experimental learning environment, not a slider-only demo or a page of notes. Where the syllabus expects practical competence, it must include the following relevant elements:

1. **Briefing:** title, purpose, scientific question, learning outcomes and prerequisites.
2. **Apparatus/materials:** selectable or illustrated apparatus, labels, purpose and correct setup.
3. **Safety:** hazards, PPE, precautions, supervision, waste/disposal and prohibited actions as appropriate.
4. **Variables:** independent, dependent and controlled variables; explain how each is changed or measured.
5. **Method:** numbered procedure, sequencing, checkpoints and learner decisions.
6. **Prediction:** learner records expected result and scientific justification before running.
7. **Execution:** controls/actions change the model in a meaningful, visible and scientifically consistent way.
8. **Observation:** learner records qualitative observations and/or readings in a lab notebook.
9. **Repeats:** repeated trials and appropriate treatment of variation where relevant.
10. **Data handling:** correctly structured tables, headings, units, decimal places and significant figures.
11. **Graphing:** suitable graph type, axes, units, scale, plotted points, best-fit line/curve and gradient/intercept interpretation as required.
12. **Analysis:** equations, calculations, substitutions, units, ratios, gradients, uncertainty or statistics where relevant.
13. **Conclusion:** directly answer the experimental question using the learner's own recorded evidence.
14. **Evaluation:** sources of error, limitations, reliability, validity, improvements and model assumptions.
15. **Assessment:** mark apparatus, method, variables, safety, observation, data, graph, calculation, conclusion and evaluation separately.
16. **Reflection:** compare prediction and result; explain what changed in the learner's understanding.

Do not force every item into experiments where it is scientifically irrelevant; document N/A with a reason. A virtual practical must not falsely imply that a student has physically handled apparatus. Teach the real-world technique and safety requirements separately.

## 5. Simulation, animation and model integrity
Every original simulation must document:
- Learning purpose and syllabus mapping.
- Scientific principle, equations and algorithm.
- Inputs, outputs, units, valid ranges and initial conditions.
- Assumptions, boundary conditions and excluded effects.
- Expected results at normal and limiting cases.
- Validation examples and automated tests.
- Accessibility and offline behaviour.

Use accurate models at the level appropriate to the learner. Explain simplifications. Label relative indices as indices, not measured rates or concentrations. Clearly identify simulated readings and never present them as real laboratory data. Random processes must not be depicted as deterministic unless the model explicitly represents an expected value. Avoid false precision.

## 6. Subject standards

### Biology
- Build progressively across organisation: molecules, organelles, cells, tissues, organs, systems, organisms, populations, communities and ecosystems where relevant.
- Teach structures with accurate labelled diagrams, relative scale and function.
- Animate dynamic processes such as transport, nutrition, respiration, excretion, reproduction, inheritance, cell division and ecological interactions where applicable.
- Include classification, identification keys, microscopy, biological drawings, specimen observation, measurement, ecological sampling, data recording and experimental design where required.
- Distinguish observation from inference and correlation from causation.
- State when a cell, organism or ecosystem model is simplified.

### Chemistry
- Build from matter, particles, atomic structure, periodicity, bonding and formulae to equations, mole concepts, energetics, kinetics, equilibrium, acids/bases, redox, electrolysis, inorganic/organic chemistry and industrial/environmental chemistry as required by the syllabus.
- Connect macroscopic observation, particle-level explanation and symbolic equations.
- Teach balancing equations, state symbols, ionic equations, formula writing, oxidation numbers, mole ratios, units and calculations step by step.
- Practical work should cover apparatus, safe reagent handling, qualitative analysis, titration, separation/purification and observation/inference where required.
- Never teach a colour test or chemical inference without conditions, expected observation and relevant limitations.

### Physics
- Build from measurement, SI units, prefixes, significant figures, uncertainty, vectors and mathematical foundations.
- Teach mechanics, matter, heat, waves, sound, light, electricity, magnetism and modern topics as specified by the syllabus.
- Connect physical meaning, diagram, equation, units, graph and experimental measurement.
- Teach vector directions, sign conventions, free-body diagrams, circuit symbols, ray diagrams, energy transfers and graph gradients explicitly.
- Practical work should include apparatus setup, alignment, repeated readings, tables, plotting, gradients/intercepts, uncertainty and evaluation as relevant.

## 7. Examination assessment standard
For each syllabus statement, assessment should include the relevant mix of:
- JAMB single-best-answer items testing recall, understanding, application, calculation, interpretation and reasoning.
- WAEC theory/structured items testing definitions, explanations, comparisons, diagrams, derivations, calculations and extended reasoning.
- WAEC practical/data items testing apparatus, method, observations, tables, graphs, calculations, deductions and evaluation.
- Mixed-topic items that test links across the syllabus.

Each item must have a unique identifier, syllabus mapping, skill/difficulty tag, correct answer, rationale and review status. Distractors must be plausible but clearly wrong for a defensible reason. Avoid ambiguity, accidental clues, duplicate questions and unbalanced answer-position patterns. Theory answers need marking points and acceptable equivalent expressions. Practical questions must use scientifically coherent data and units.

## 8. Mastery, remediation and progression
Track separate skill dimensions: prerequisite knowledge, recall, conceptual explanation, visual interpretation, calculation, practical method, data/graph analysis and examination performance. Do not reduce mastery to lesson completion or a single aggregate score.

A suggested configurable starting gate is at least 80% in each essential skill domain, with no critical safety or method error. Validate thresholds through learner pilots; they are not a promise of exam results. A failed domain triggers targeted explanation, a different visual/example, guided practice and fresh equivalent assessment. Do not simply repeat the same question. Revisit weak skills with spaced retrieval and cumulative review.

## 9. Accessibility, usability and offline requirements
- Core learning content and essential activities should work offline wherever practical.
- External resources are optional enrichment, never a dependency for core syllabus coverage.
- Support readable text, scalable layouts, accessible labels, contrast, keyboard/screen-reader semantics where feasible and alternatives to colour-only meaning.
- Keep controls usable on small Android screens and provide clear instructions and feedback.
- Avoid unnecessary battery, memory and network use.
- Persist meaningful learner progress safely and preserve existing progress/database compatibility.

## 10. Content rights and attribution
Use original assets or content with permissions/licences compatible with the app's use. Record creator, source, licence/terms, attribution and permitted use. Do not copy paid simulations, textbook pages, diagrams, videos or question banks without permission. When a third-party resource cannot be embedded or redistributed, create an original explanatory equivalent rather than copying its protected content.

## 11. Quality assurance and release states
Before release, check syllabus accuracy, scientific/mathematical correctness, completeness of instruction, visual labels, model equations and boundary cases, practical safety, question validity, answer keys, accessibility, offline behaviour, performance and regression of existing features.

Use these statuses:
- **Audited:** syllabus statement and requirements checked against a current official source.
- **Implemented:** required teaching and learning experiences are present.
- **Assessed:** aligned objective/theory/practical assessments are present and reviewed.
- **Mastery-ready:** skill tracking, pass criteria and remediation operate.
- **Verified:** academic review and technical tests pass with evidence.

A topic is not complete until all applicable statuses are verified. Subject completion requires every syllabus statement to be traceable and verified, with no unresolved critical gaps. Report real coverage and remaining gaps honestly.

## 12. Engineering preservation
Keep the current Kotlin/Jetpack Compose architecture unless a documented design review justifies a change. Preserve existing navigation, local database, progress, assessments, question banks, practicals and the wider seven-subject scope. Add tests for calculation engines, topic mappings, boundary cases and regressions. Run unit tests and Android build for every code batch. Report only the CI status for the exact commit being discussed. Do not claim physical-device testing unless performed.

## 13. Required learning design principle
The learner should repeatedly **see it, hear/read it, predict it, manipulate it, observe it, explain it, practise it, retrieve it and apply it**. The app must create understanding, not merely expose information.

You may study and reproduce structural patterns such as lesson pillars, navigation, animation placement, illustration cards, practical-lab workspaces, activity flows, feedback patterns and visual hierarchy. **Never copy, import, paraphrase or adapt its course-content write-ups, explanations, worked examples, question wording, answers, scenarios, experiment instructions, marking points or other teaching prose.** All MeritPrep 2.0 academic content must be independently authored, scientifically checked and mapped to its own JAMB/WAEC syllabus evidence. Do not modify the reference repository.
