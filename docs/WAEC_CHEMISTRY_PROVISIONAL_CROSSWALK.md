## WAEC Chemistry practical specification

The working reference describes Paper 3 as three compulsory practical questions, with quantitative analysis, qualitative analysis and familiarity with suggested practical activities. It distinguishes the school practical from alternative-to-practical for private candidates.

Required practical coverage to map and test:

1. **General skills:** mass/volume measurement; preparation and dilution of standard solutions; filtration, recrystallisation and melting-point determination; heats of neutralisation/solution; pH by colorimetry; rate curves; simple equilibrium constants.
2. **Quantitative analysis:** acid–base titration; concentrations; molar masses; water of crystallisation; solubility/purity; stoichiometry; redox titrations including acidic permanganate with Fe²⁺, acidic permanganate with oxalate and iodine/thiosulfate.
3. **Qualitative analysis:** cations NH₄⁺, Ca²⁺, Pb²⁺, Cu²⁺, Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺, Al³⁺ and Zn²⁺; anions Cl⁻, SO₃²⁻, CO₃²⁻, NO₃⁻ and SO₄²⁻; halogen displacement; gas tests H₂, NH₃, CO₂, HCl and SO₂; functional-group tests for alkenes, alkanols, alkanoic acids, sugars, starch and proteins.

## Current implementation observations

- The app already has a Chemistry qualitative guide for cations, anions and gases, with safety notes and confirmatory-test cautions.
- A guided acid–base titration model and a stoichiometrically balanced acidic permanganate/iron(II) redox model are embedded in lessons.
- A separate chemistry separation-method selector, mole-ratio/limiting-reagent exploration, calorimetry calculation, periodic-trend selector, industrial-process comparison and metal-extraction comparison are present.
- The lesson activities are not yet proof that every practical skill has a complete apparatus-to-evaluation virtual lab.
- A gas test shown in a simulation must never encourage a learner to prepare or inhale hazardous gases.
